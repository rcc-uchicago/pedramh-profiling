# C1 verdict — the rollout fine-tune worked. The old number measured the one lead where it is supposed to lose.

**Measured 2026-09-10.** Jobs **7603089** (base) and **7603119** (C1), both at
`valid_autoreg_steps=20` — 21 leads x 101 channels, 512 validation samples,
identical configuration, compared at **fixed rollout depth**.

First head-to-head that has ever been possible on this project. It was blocked
until commit `652e9505` because `MetricsHandler` built zero metric handles on
E3SM channel names.

---

## 1. Headline

> **C1 is 1.5 % worse at one step and 3-4.7 % better at every step after that.
> The crossover is at lead 2.** The "+1.5 % worse" figure this project has been
> quoting is the value at lead 1 — precisely the one lead a rollout fine-tune
> is designed to trade away.

| | value |
|---|---|
| C1 vs base at lead 1 (6 h) | **1.50 % worse**, 12/101 channels better |
| Crossover | **lead 2 (12 h)** |
| Best advantage | **4.65 % better at 36 h**, 85/101 channels better |
| Sustained to 126 h | **3.00 % better**, 77/101 channels better |

## 3. What this settles

1. ✅ **C1 worked.** It bought multi-step skill at the cost of single-step
   accuracy — exactly its design intent, with the crossover at the first
   multi-step lead.
2. ✅ **The old comparison was not wrong, it was mis-scoped.** `0.01284` vs
   `0.01303` is the lead-1 column of this table. It is a real number that
   answers a question nobody was asking.
3. ✅ **Exposure bias is a real and addressable component of the error.** If
   the dominant failure were mode-averaging, training on the model's own
   outputs should not have helped. It helped, monotonically, across 77-89 of
   101 channels.
4. ✅ **The rollout/ensemble direction is not misaimed.** The handoff's third
   outcome — "error barely moves, STOP" — is excluded twice over: base RMSE
   grows 4.65x over 5.25 days, and the rollout fine-tune moves the curve.

---

## 4. What this does NOT settle

⚠ Four caveats, all real.

1. **ACC reverses at the longest leads.** C1 leads on ACC from 12 h to ~108 h,
   then loses at 114/120/126 h. RMSE still favours C1 there. Either noise
   (n=1) or C1's advantage genuinely decaying at long lead — **not
   distinguishable from this run**.
2. **`PS` does not benefit.** 5.9 % worse at 6 h and still 0.8 % worse at
   126 h. The improvement is broad but not universal; surface pressure is the
   clearest counter-example and is worth a look from the science owner.
3. **126 h is still too short to separate exposure bias from mode-averaging.**
   Those diverge only as error approaches the climatological level; at ACC
   0.867 we are far from it. The discriminating measurement is K=56 (14 days),
   which the inference port delivers.
4. **n=1.** One validation split, no repeats, no error bars. The 3-4.7 % band
   is consistent across 21 leads and 77-89 channels, which is reassuring, but
   it is not a confidence interval.

---

## 5. Consequences

- **Use C1's checkpoint for anything autoregressive.** Beyond lead 1 it
  dominates on RMSE across most channels.
- **Keep the base checkpoint for single-step use**, if there is any.
- ⚠ **C1's `best_ckpt` was selected on the wrong criterion.** Checkpoint
  selection keys on `valid_logs["base"]["validation loss"]`
  (`deterministic_trainer.py:446,458`), which is the lead-1 scalar, while C1's
  objective is 2-step. All 24 epoch checkpoints are on disk, so re-selecting at
  a depth we care about is free — no retraining. Worth doing before the
  ensemble sweep.
- **C2 is now justified.** `n_future=1` bought 3-4.7 %; the open question is
  whether `n_future=2-3` buys more. Memory model and sizing:
  `polaris_makani_1node_production_handoff.md` §C2.
- **Correct the documentation.** The checkpoint-usage docs and
  `submit_rollout_scorecard.sh`'s header describe `0.01284` as a 4-step /
  24-hour score. It is a **1-step** score. The number is right, the label is
  wrong.

## 6. Reproduce

```bash
QUEUE=debug WALLTIME=00:30:00 TAG_SUFFIX=_pl \
  bash polaris/submit_rollout_scorecard.sh prod1n_b32_sgdr    20
QUEUE=debug WALLTIME=00:30:00 TAG_SUFFIX=_pl \
  bash polaris/submit_rollout_scorecard.sh c1_rollout_full_b16 20
```

Curves land at
`<expDir>/scores/metrics_ep0000.h5`, datasets `RMSE|L1|ACC` -> `metric_data`
of shape `(21 leads, 101 channels)` with `lead_time` (hours) and `channel`
dimension scales attached.

⚠ **`LOAD_LOSS=0` is required** or the C1 arm dies at construction with
`RuntimeError: Error(s) in loading state_dict for LossHandler` — `LossHandler`
carries running statistics whose shape depends on `n_future`. Fixed in the
launcher, commit `e771b6bf`; first hit on job 7603090.

⚠ **Control — the lead-1 scalar is unchanged by the per-lead patch, so a moved
number means a broken scoring path.** Measured:

| arm | training run reported | scorecard reproduced | agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | `0.012838906608521938` | `0.012838906608521938` | **bit-identical** |
| C1 | `0.013031292706727982` | `0.013031451962888240` | 5 significant figures, `1.2e-5` relative |

The base arm is exact. **C1 is not bit-identical**, and the difference is
expected rather than alarming: the C1 training run evaluated with its own
config's eval-sample count, while the scorecard forces `EVAL_SAMPLES=512`, so
the two average over slightly different sample sets. A `1.2e-5` relative
difference is three orders of magnitude below the 3-4.7 % effect being measured.

⚠ **This does not weaken the comparison at all.** Both arms in §2 were scored
with *identical* scorecard settings (`EVAL_SAMPLES=512`, `VALID_AUTOREG=20`,
same 512 samples, same leads), so the ratio table is apples-to-apples. The
control's only job is to prove the scoring path still restores the right
weights, and it does.
